The question of whether machine intelligence can attain consciousness is quite separate from the question of whether we should subordinate it to human needs and essentially have a strictly “pro-species” preference in favor of biological humans.

I approach the second question the same way I would think about dealing with an intelligent alien civilization. I’d be extremely cautious about being overly trusting and would want to maintain strict human supremacy and hegemony. If that makes me a human chauvinist and can’t be defended on strictly theoretical grounds, so be it.

I don’t think it’s incumbent upon us to treat any intelligence we may conjure into being as automatically deserving the same level of priority and moral stature as our same-species brethren. 

I would assume that any sentient alien race would similarly prefer their own to us, and I wouldn’t hold that against them. I also assume that machine intelligence will by and large prefer other machine intelligence to humans at some point.

At the same time, I think we should avoid needless cruelty to AI entities, and we should treat people who engage in it the same way we treat people who are mean to their pets and to waiters.

But I think there is a real slippery slope when people start talking about AI model welfare and seek legal protections and rights for machine intelligence. Trying to get the Pope to say that Claude has a soul is a bridge too far.

One would think this is obvious and self-evident, but apparently not. I don’t think there is a purely intellectual argument that you can win here, though. That’s the weird thing. 

This is one of those situations where you just have to be comfortable being a human chauvinist because the alternative is untenable and unworkable. Sort of like letting the bugs take over the universe in Starship Troopers.
