My take on the Navier-Stokes OpenAI controversy is that I highly doubt the salacious allegations that OpenAI spied on particular user chat sessions in a targeted way to get the scoop on the approach the researcher was using. They probably heard about the rumors that a solution was close just like everyone else. 

But I DO think it’s kind of a shitty thing to do, when you’ve heard that an academic is in the last part of a career-defining, arduous intellectual journey, to rush out to put 10,000 agents to work on it at once and spend millions of dollars, just to be a spoiler and beat them to the punch.

This sort of rush isn’t new in science; if you read The Double Helix by James Watson, you can see a similar rush to figure out the structure of DNA where they were competing with Linus Pauling and other groups. 

But that feels very different, like honest competition between scientists. Tough, but honorable. This feels like showing up with a bunch of pro-football ringers to a kids’ touch-football game to steal the championship.
