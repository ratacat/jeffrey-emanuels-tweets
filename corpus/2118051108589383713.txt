### 1. The Smoking Gun in Your Extracted Play:5 Gen 1 Firmware

When we inspect rootfs-extracted on the 32 MB Play:5 Gen 1:

• The actual kernel audio driver (audiodev.o) is only 77 KB! And it isn't even stripped: it initializes the Cirrus Logic CS44800 8-channel PWM amplifier controller over I2C and exposes clean character devices (/dev/dsp_dev for PowerPC MPC8260 SCC DMA rings and /dev/audioctl for buttons, Line-In detect, LED PWM, and thermal protection).
• Meanwhile, anacapad + lib is 7.9 MB on disk and eats ~21.5 MB of your 32 MB RAM because it carries 50+ legacy SMAPI music service clients (Rhapsody, Napster, Audible, Pandora, SiriusXM...), SMBv1/CIFS network share indexers (/tmp/smb), a dozen 2008 audio codecs (WMA, Audible .aa, RealAudio, MP3, SBC...), and huge UPnP/SOAP/DIDL-Lite XML DOM trees.
• Because anacapad eats ~21.5 MB of the 32 MB RAM, the Play:5 Gen 1 has < 1.5 MB left for network jitter buffers—which is why Spotify Connect and multi-room grouping constantly drop out on Wi-Fi hiccups, and why Sonos segregated S1 from S2.

### 2. What Happens When We Strip Everything Except Your 5 Requirements

By building a single static Rust binary (franken-speaker, ~1.15 MB on disk, ~2.8 MB runtime RAM) on top of asupersync + frankenlibc + franken_remote with only 3 tiny pure-Rust decoders (Opus for the internal mesh, Ogg Vorbis for Spotify, and AAC/ALAC for AirPlay):

1. Dropout-Proof Spotify & Unified S1+S2 Grouping:
    • Freed from anacapad, even your oldest 32 MB Play:5 Gen 1 gets an 8–12 MB lock-free RAM ring buffer (over 3 minutes of 320 kbps Spotify lookahead buffered in RAM within 2 seconds of pressing play!).
    • Every speaker (Play:5 Gen 1, Playbar, Play:1, Play:3, Sub, Sonos One) runs the identical protocol—so S1 and S2 segregation completely disappears.
    • Multi-room audio uses asupersync's RFC 6330 RaptorQ fountain codes over 48 kHz Opus/PCM: if a 2.4 GHz microwave burst drops 15% of packets to a speaker, it reconstructs the missing audio frames mathematically with zero TCP retransmission stalls.
2. AirPlay 2 on Every Vintage Speaker:
    • A ~250 KB pure-Rust AirPlay 2 / RAOP receiver makes every room and group (including your 2010 Play:5 Gen 1s and Play:1s that Sonos never gave AirPlay to) appear natively in your iPhone Control Center AirPlay picker.

    • Dynamic Audio-Latency Reporting: The speaker continuously calculates its exact sample queue depth (N_{jitter} + N_{raptorq} + N_{dsp} + N_{dma}) and reports it in the AirPlay RTP/RTSP timing channel (so iPhone/iPad/macOS automatically delays YouTube video frames to match the exact millisecond) and over WebSocket to a lightweight browser/desktop
    helper.

    • Pure-Rust userspace WireGuard (< 1.8 MB RAM) + your 1 GB Sonos One (192.168.6.130) and 256 MB Play:1 A200s acting as redundant Tailnet service routers give every room its own MagicDNS address (https://t.co/1qC6eoUe1g, etc.) with zero Go tailscaled RAM bloat.
