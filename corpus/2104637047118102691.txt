If you invested in the IPO of the original Suez Canal Company in 1858, you would’ve made around a 6.5% money-weighted IRR if you held for 20 years and then sold it. 

But you would’ve hit a nasty 80% drawdown before then when the company almost went bust and then during the Franco-Prussian War.

What’s a lot more unusual is that, if you held for 50 years, your money-weighted IRR was much higher, at around 9%! You don’t tend to see that.
