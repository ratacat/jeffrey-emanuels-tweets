I was showing my 3 young daughters the 1985 movie Anne of Green Gables last night and was struck by how much it felt like various Miyazaki films. Both the visuals of the landscapes, the dress and appearance of the characters, and even the music.

Apparently, this Canadian made-for-TV movie was wildly popular in Japan, to the point where it ran in theaters for 5 years! There are tons of Japanese people who visit tiny Prince Edward's Island every year because of their fascination and obsession with this movie.

Anyway, I decided to ask Claude about it, and it doesn't seem to be widely commented about. But Miyazaki literally worked on an Anne of Green Gables show himself in 1979, so he almost certainly saw it around the time when he was making Totoro and Kikki's Delivery Service.

I highly recommend the movie if you have kids (it's hard to find but you can buy it on Amazon streaming). It's good for them to see what actual childhood adversity looks like (e.g., being a rejected orphan who nobody wants) so they feel less aggrieved by their own day-to-day slights involving toys and sibling squabbles.
